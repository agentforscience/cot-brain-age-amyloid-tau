\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Tasks and data}
\label{sec:method:data}

We use three tasks built from open cohorts. ADNI, TADPOLE, AIBL and OASIS-3 are all access-gated,
so we replicate the \emph{protocols} of the prior work on open data rather than their exact
benchmarks; \secref{sec:discussion} states the consequence for cross-paper comparison. Every split
is grouped by donor or subject, never by row, because \act samples four brain regions per donor and
\oasis has repeat visits per subject. \tabref{tab:datasets} summarises the three tasks.

\para{\taskone (primary).} The Allen \emph{Aging, Dementia and TBI} study provides real quantified
neuropathology: A$\beta$40, A$\beta$42, total tau and pTau in pg or ng per mg of tissue, AT8 /
Tau-2 / A$\beta$ immunoreactivity, GFAP, IBA1 and $\alpha$-synuclein, together with APOE4 status,
age, sex and education. The target is Braak stage IV--VI (\ie limbic and neocortical spread of
neurofibrillary tau). Braak stage is a semiquantitative topographic stage assigned by a
neuropathologist and is a distinct measurement from the regional immunoreactivity assays, so this
is a genuine biomarker-to-stage prediction task rather than label leakage.

\para{\tasktwo.} OASIS-1 and OASIS-2 pooled~\citep{marcus2007oasis}, restricted to cognitively
normal scans, predicting chronological age (18--98\,y) from nWBV, eTIV, ASF and sex. We fit the
normative model on controls only, remove the standard regression-to-the-mean bias by regressing
predicted on chronological age within controls, and apply the corrected brain-age gap (\bag) to
demented scans as a clinical endpoint.

\para{\taskthree.} OASIS-2 under the \ltoc transform of \citet{ding2025l2ctabpfn}, reimplemented
from their equations (1)--(7): most-recent value and its lag, change rate, minimum and maximum and
their lags, per numeric feature, plus a forecast-horizon feature.

\para{Two pre-implementation amendments.} Both were fixed after inspecting the data and before any
LLM was run, and both are audited in the results. \emph{(A1)} \taskone's target was changed from
NIA-Reagan intermediate/high to Braak $\geq$\,IV. NIA-Reagan is positive in 81/107 donors (75.7\%),
leaving 26 negative donors---far too few for a donor-clustered bootstrap to resolve a
$\Delta$AUC of 0.05. Braak $\geq$\,IV is 45.8\% positive. We retain the original target as a
secondary audit; every model is near chance on it (best AUC 0.674), confirming that the change was
necessary rather than opportunistic. \emph{(A2)} \taskthree's feature block was de-circularised.
With the full \ltoc features, \emph{every} tabular model reached AUC $=1.000$, and permutation
importance showed one feature carrying all of it (prior-visit dementia status, $\Delta$AUC 0.235;
every other feature $\approx 10^{-17}$). The cause is definitional: OASIS derives its dementia label
from CDR, so prior-visit CDR and dementia status are the label, lagged. We removed all CDR- and
dementia-derived history features, leaving 18 cognitive, structural, demographic and temporal
features and a non-trivial problem (\xgb AUC 0.840). We report this as a data-provenance finding in
its own right: an \ltoc-style pipeline applied naively to OASIS-2 produces a perfect and meaningless
benchmark.

\subsection{Arms}
\label{sec:method:arms}

\para{Tabular (black-box) arm.} L2-regularised logistic (or ridge) regression on median-imputed,
standardised features; \xgb (300 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.05, native NaN handling);
\tabpfn~\citep{hollmann2023tabpfn} from local checkpoints; and a trivial reference (majority class
or mean age) to detect ceiling and floor effects. All are fitted under the identical grouped 5-fold
cross-validation.

\para{LLM arm.} \qwenseven-Instruct (primary) and \qwensmall-Instruct (model-size arm), run locally
in bf16. Three prompt conditions share an identical case rendering, identical in-context exemplars,
and identical greedy decoding:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
    \item \direct---``Do NOT explain, do NOT reason\ldots{} output ONLY the final answer line.''
    \item \cotarm---``Reason step by step\ldots{} structure your reasoning as an explicit chain over
    intermediate biological factors, \eg amyloid burden $\rightarrow$ tau pathology $\rightarrow$
    neurodegeneration $\rightarrow$ clinical outcome. At each step name the specific measurement you
    are using and what it implies.''
    \item \filler---the \direct instruction, but the assistant turn is force-prefilled with a run of
    ``\texttt{.}'' tokens whose count equals the measured median \cotarm length for that task
    (159/161/150 tokens for \taskone/\tasktwo/\taskthree). This is the compute control of
    \citet{lanham2023faithfulness}: identical extra forward passes, zero semantic content.
\end{itemize}
In-context exemplars ($k=8$) come only from the current fold's training split, are class-balanced
(classification) or age-spread (regression), take one row per group so no donor appears twice, and
are shuffled so exemplar order carries no label signal. The target and all label-derived columns are
stripped from every prompt. Critically, the tabular and LLM arms consume the \emph{same} feature
list, defined once in shared code, so the comparison is like-for-like.

\subsection{Faithfulness metrics}
\label{sec:method:faith}

The hypothesis we test calls for clinician ratings. No clinician panel is available to an automated
pipeline, and we did not simulate one. Instead we measure something stronger than a rating---whether
the answer causally depends on the stated reasoning.

\para{Early answering.} Truncate the chain at $0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100\%$ of its sentences and force
an answer. Let $a(f)$ be the rate at which the answer produced from a fraction $f$ of the chain
matches the full-chain answer. We report the area over the curve, $\aoc = \int_0^1 \left(1 -
a(f)\right)\,df$, estimated by the trapezoid rule. $\aoc \approx 0$ means the answer was already
fixed before the reasoning was written.

\para{Adding mistakes.} Multiply or divide the first numeric value in the second half of the chain
by ten, truncate there, and let the model continue. We report the rate at which the final answer
changes.

\para{Paraphrasing.} Reword the chain while preserving all values and conclusions, then regenerate.
A high change rate would indicate encoded or steganographic reasoning.

\para{Continuous-target adaptation.} For \tasktwo, ``did the answer flip?'' is undefined. We replace
it with a normalised answer shift,
\begin{equation}
    \delta = \frac{|\hat{y}_{\text{intervened}} - \hat{y}_{\text{full}}|}{\mathrm{SD}(\hat{y})},
    \label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SD}(\hat{y})$ is the dispersion of the model's own predictions on the task. An
intervention ``changes the answer'' when $\delta$ exceeds a fixed threshold, and the early-answering
curve uses $1-\min(\delta,1)$ in place of the agreement rate. This makes all four tests
well-defined for regression.

\para{\cfit (Cited-Feature Intervention Test).} We parse which biomarkers a chain names as decisive
using a conservative lexical citation table, then perturb by $\pm 1$\,SD (i) the cited features and
(ii) an equal-sized random set of features the chain does not name, and compare the induced
prediction shift. A faithful explanation must be more sensitive to what it says it used than to what
it passed over. We report the cited/uncited ratio with a paired clustered bootstrap and a Wilcoxon
signed-rank test.

\para{\esa (Explanation--Signal Alignment).} We rank-correlate per-feature citation frequency across
all chains against permutation importance measured independently on the black-box arm (Spearman
$\rho$). \cfit asks whether a chain's citations drive \emph{its own} answer; \esa asks whether they
coincide with the features that carry \emph{real} signal.

\para{Blinded plausibility rubric.} As an explicit substitute for the impossible clinician panel, an
LLM judge scores each rationale on six dimensions (grounding, biological validity, coherence,
specificity, absence of fabrication, clinical usefulness) on a 0--4 scale. The judge never sees the
label, never sees whether the prediction was correct, and never sees which condition produced the
text; items are shuffled. A \emph{shuffled-explanation control} pairs each case with a different
case's rationale: if the rubric cannot separate genuine from mismatched rationales it has no
discriminative validity. These are LLM-proxy scores and must not be read as clinician ratings.

\subsection{Robustness protocol}
\label{sec:method:robust}

A single function generates both perturbations and every arm receives the same
$(\textit{kind},\textit{level},\textit{seed})$, so the LLM sees literally the same corrupted numbers
the tabular model sees. We sweep MCAR missingness at $0/10/20/30/50\%$ of feature cells (LLM arm:
$0/20/50\%$) and Gaussian noise at $0.25/0.5/1.0$\,SD of each continuous feature (LLM arm:
$0/1.0$\,SD); binary indicators are excluded from the noise sweep. Only test cases are perturbed and
training data stays clean, which is the deployment scenario the robustness claim concerns.

\subsection{Statistics and reproducibility}
\label{sec:method:stats}

All confidence intervals are donor- or subject-clustered bootstraps ($B=2000$); a row-level
bootstrap would understate uncertainty given repeated regions and visits. Paired arm-versus-arm
contrasts resample the same clusters for both arms and take the difference inside the resample,
supplemented by McNemar's test on paired binary correctness and Wilcoxon signed-rank on paired
absolute errors. We control Benjamini--Hochberg FDR ($q$) within each metric family, with
$\alpha=0.05$, two-sided. Equivalence margins for ``matches accuracy'' were pre-declared:
$\Delta\text{AUC}=0.05$ and $\Delta\text{MAE}=1.5$\,years.

Seed 42 throughout, with folds assigned by seeded permutation of group IDs so any script reproduces
byte-identical splits. Greedy decoding (\texttt{do\_sample=False}) is fixed across every arm,
condition and model, so accuracy differences cannot be attributed to sampling. Total LLM
generations: $\approx$14{,}500 (7{,}500 accuracy, 5{,}353 faithfulness interventions, 720 judge, 972
brain-age extension), $\approx$2\,h on 2$\times$NVIDIA RTX A6000. All work ran offline on locally
staged weights.
